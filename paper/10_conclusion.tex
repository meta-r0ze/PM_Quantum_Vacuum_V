\section{Conclusion and Remaining Derivations}
The routing model organizes proposed mass assignments and conditional decay laws. Its mathematical core is precise: allowed transitions preserve specified labels while lowering latency; nonnegative partial widths add; a memoryless hazard gives exponential survival; and a conditional scaling bridge gives a fifth power if the specified kinetic, vertex and rate dimensions are obtained internally. The accompanying Lean project checks these implications.

The draft benchmarks have nonuniform agreement. The tau, charm-sector and kaon entries do not support a blanket claim of agreement within reported uncertainties. Each comparison must specify the physical state, mass convention, channels, corrections and uncertainty before precision testing.

The principal open task is a microscopic transition operator selecting exits and amplitudes. It must produce the waiting-time law and justify the proposed phase-space and channel weights. Absolute proton stability, contextual neutrino masses, a Yang--Mills mass gap and a gravitational interpretation of closure remain physical conjectures requiring additional arguments.

The external weak-interaction calculation is a comparison benchmark. Deriving its operator and measure from the substrate remains essential to an independent dual account. The formal $E_8$ counterexample shows why symmetry and root normalization alone cannot close this gap.
