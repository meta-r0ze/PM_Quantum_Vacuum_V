\begin{abstract}
All persistent systems must process state transitions within finite capacity limits. When internal complexity or transition frequency exceeds this threshold, the system can no longer dissipate entropy against discrete state allocations without losing structural information. Rather than collapsing stochastically, the system undergoes deterministic re-routing into a lower-complexity protocol, governed by interface aperture and thermodynamic limits. We identify this as the universal mechanics of decay.

We validate this framework on the $E_8$ geometric substrate. First, we derive charge, color, spin, mass, and lifetime, mapping the routing protocols for leptons and quarks. Second, we identify composite protocols as baryons and mesons. We then show that decay lifetimes spanning eighteen orders of magnitude naturally follow routing rules dictated by transmission dimensionality. From fundamental masses to decay lifetimes, all predictions match experimental measurements to within reported uncertainties, with no continuous free parameters. Finally, this geometry yields the proton radius, explains the neutron lifetime anomaly, and derives the strong CP problem, the hierarchy problem, and the Yang--Mills mass gap as geometric corollaries, while offering a falsifiable prediction: neutrino masses must follow a strict normal hierarchy.
\end{abstract}
