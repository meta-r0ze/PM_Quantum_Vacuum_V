\section{Phenomenological Interpretation and Limits}
\label{sec:observer_effect}

\subsection{Mass conventions and proposed routing effects}
The ledger uses the coordinate factor $\eta=171/172$ to propose a relation $M_{\rm obs}=M_{\rm bare}/\eta$ for selected quark assignments. This is a model hypothesis. Relating it to a renormalized quark mass requires a specified scheme, scale and matching calculation. A scheme-dependent mass is not, by itself, evidence that a detector changes a particle's invariant mass.

Likewise, a charged composite's self-energy must be calculated using its electromagnetic structure. A small residual between a geometric mass formula and a measured mass does not alone identify a radiation correction or a discrete lattice error.

\subsection{Proposed neutrino routing scale}
The draft proposes two scales, $M_{\rm rest}\simeq9.2$ meV and
\begin{equation}
M_{\rm weak}=(\sigma+1)M_{\rm rest}\simeq55.2\ \mathrm{meV}.
\end{equation}
This is a conditional scale assignment, not a derived neutrino propagation Hamiltonian. A cosmological upper bound on a mass sum and an oscillation constraint on squared-mass differences are different observables. An upper bound of $120$ meV and a characteristic oscillation scale near $50$ meV are not contradictory merely because they differ.

For the draft's illustrative values,
\begin{equation}
(55.2\ \mathrm{meV})^2-(1.2\ \mathrm{meV})^2
\simeq3.0456\times10^{-3}\ \mathrm{eV}^2.
\end{equation}
This is approximately $22\%$ above the draft's reference $2.5\times10^{-3}\ \mathrm{eV}^2$. It should not be described as a precision derivation or a resolution of a cosmological discrepancy. Lean certifies the gap: with $M_{\rm rest}$ at the thermal floor, $M_{\rm weak}^2-M_{\rm rest}^2$ lies in $(2.967,2.969)\times10^{-3}$~eV$^2$, more than $17\sigma$ above NuFIT~6.0's $(2.534\pm0.025)\times10^{-3}$~eV$^2$, and the multiplier that would fit is $5.55$--$5.57$, not $6$ (\texttt{NeutrinoContext}). A three-state mass and mixing model, its propagation law, and a common likelihood analysis are still needed.

A normal ordering may be adopted as a prediction of a specified resonance assignment. Demonstrating that no inverted assignment is possible requires a completeness argument for the permitted states; the channel count alone does not provide it.

\subsection{An atomic-scale consistency relation}
Given $\alpha=1/Z_{\rm geo}$, the usual Bohr-radius relation can be rewritten as
\begin{equation}
a_0=\frac{\hbar}{\alpha m_ec}
=\frac{\hbar Z_{\rm geo}}{m_ec}.
\end{equation}
This is a useful consistency identity once the physical interpretation of $Z_{\rm geo}$ and the electron mass is supplied. It does not independently derive those inputs or an atomic spectrum from the routing rules.
